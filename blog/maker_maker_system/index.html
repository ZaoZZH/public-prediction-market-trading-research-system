Building the maker/maker trading system

The architecture behind the real-money test of the Kalshi vs Polymarket series: two venue engines, a single-threaded decision core, automatic fixture matching, book freshness, a trading record built for the replay comparison, and a small cloud deployment.

System architecture of maker/maker, a three-order cross-venue market maker for soccer team-win markets on Kalshi and Polymarket: components, fixture discovery, order-book data flow, the decision and reconciliation loop, safety controls, recording and deployment.
Author

Zehao Zhou

Published

October 8, 2026

Part 3 of the Kalshi vs Polymarket series ended with a strategy outline and a replay against recorded order books. Three resting orders per team-win outcome, a Polymarket YES bid, a Polymarket YES ask and a Kalshi YES ask, made money in the replay even at the back of every queue. This article describes the system built to run that outline with real orders.

The first live test has a narrower job than the replay. At the test size of L = 10 contracts, the replay makes about $2.57 per club fixture (95% interval 2.17–3.01), loses on 31% of fixtures, and has a worst fixture of −$17. About 25–30 fixtures are needed before an interval excludes zero. That replay merges Polymarket pairs back into cash at once, which the first live version does not, so these figures are a guide rather than a forecast. The first fixtures are therefore a measurement of what the replay had to assume: queue position, double fills, latency and holding time. The system records those quantities as carefully as it trades.

Summary

  1. Four processes from one image. Two venue engines behind one gRPC contract, an execution host for the strategy, and a management service for the control page, linked by mutual TLS (section 2).
  2. Fixtures are matched automatically; enabling one is the human check. Both venues’ moneyline listings in six club competitions are paired every 10 minutes (section 3).
  3. A book is either fresh or pulled. Engines re-send every valid book once a second while its socket is provably alive; execution cancels an outcome’s three orders when either book is 3 s old (section 4).
  4. The rules are a pure function; everything else is reconciliation. One strategy thread owns all state, computes the desired orders from books with its own orders removed, and issues the fewest writes to get there: an amend on Kalshi, a cancel and re-post on Polymarket (section 5).
  5. Uncertainty stops quoting before it can change inventory. Unknown order states, stale books, unhealthy engines, missing heartbeats and a loss stop all pull orders, and every start reads inventory from the venues (section 6).
  6. The record is built for the replay comparison. Decisions, order events, queue estimates, books, trades and fill windows go to one SQLite file per day, so the replay can be rerun on exactly the fixtures traded (section 7).
  7. It runs for about $5 a week on four small Fly.io machines. The engines’ memory growth on the first deployment came from allocator arenas, not a leak (section 9).

1. From replay to live orders

The replay of part 3 is a model with simplifications. Each simplification becomes a requirement of the live system:

Concern Replay (part 3, section 6) Live system
Market data Recorded snapshots, always present Sockets can stall silently and lose messages; books carry an age and are pulled when stale (section 4)
Own orders Never in the book Shown in the book; prices are read with our own size removed (section 4)
Order changes Take effect at the next second Round trips, requests in flight and unknown outcomes; a reconciler tracks the state of every order (section 5)
Fixtures A curated, matched list Matched live from both venues’ listings, re-checked every 10 minutes (section 3)
Polymarket pairs A YES + NO pair merges back to $1 at once No merges in v1: pairs stay locked until settlement and count against the cash cap
Queue position An assumed share ρ of the displayed size Measured per order, and checked against Kalshi’s own queue positions (section 7)
Inventory at start Zero Read from venue positions at every start and restart (section 6)
Failures None Stale books, engine restarts, lost fill streams, process death (section 6)

The strategy, as configured for the test

Each team-win outcome of a fixture runs on its own. The only rule shared by the two outcomes is the cash cap.

Leg Order Price Size Rests only while
pm_yes Polymarket buy YES, post-only b, Polymarket best bid min(L − q, 3L) b < Kalshi best ask (the guard)
pm_no Polymarket buy NO at 1 − a, post-only a, Polymarket best ask min(L + q, 3L) a > Kalshi best bid (the guard)
k_no Kalshi buy NO at 1 − ak, post-only ak, Kalshi best ask min(L + q, 3L) ak > Kalshi best bid, and ak − b − Kalshi maker fee > 0

Inventory q is Polymarket YES held minus NO held on either venue, per outcome. All legs also need both books valid and fresh, a Polymarket mid of 5–95 c, and the in-play window from kickoff to 104 minutes. The v1 configuration is part 3’s best replay variant with L = 10, a cash cap of $300 per venue and fixture, and no guard buffer. Every value is a configuration field, changed one at a time, between fixtures.

2. Components and topology

The four processes are built from one container image; an environment variable selects which one starts. Each has one responsibility, and the boundaries are where the failure handling sits:

Process Owns Does not
engine-pm Polymarket market and user WebSockets, books, public trades and fills, EIP-712 order signing, orders, positions, the venue-side dead-man heartbeat, Gamma market listings Make trading decisions
engine-kalshi Kalshi WebSocket (books, trades, fills on one socket), RSA request signing, orders including amend, positions, queue positions, market listings Make trading decisions
execution One compiled-in strategy module per process. maker_maker: fixture matching, decisions, order lifecycle, safety rules, the trading record Talk to a trading venue directly
management Login, the control page, configuration pass-through, a management heartbeat to execution every 15 s Hold trading state

The engines implement one gRPC service, VenueEngine. Execution never handles venue identifiers. It registers internal instruments such as <fixture>-home-pm-yes and <fixture>-home-k-no, and each engine maps them to a Polymarket token or a Kalshi ticker and side. Prices, sizes and cash are fixed-point integers (price × 10⁴, contracts × 10⁶, USD × 10⁶), so no floating-point value reaches an order. maker_maker uses these calls:

Group Calls Used for
Discovery ListSoccerMoneylineMarkets, RegisterInstruments Matching every 10 minutes; routes for enabled fixtures
Market data StreamOrderbook, StreamTrades, GetOrderbookSnapshots Trading books; queue counting and the record; display prices every 45 s
Orders PlaceOrder, CancelOrder, AmendOrder (Kalshi), GetOpenOrders Quoting, resolving unknown orders, sweeps
Account StreamFills, GetPositions, GetBalance, GetQueuePositions (Kalshi) Inventory, start-up state, queue measurement
Safety HealthCheck, RefreshDeadManSwitch (Polymarket) Engine health every 5 s; venue-side cancel when execution goes silent

engine-pm runs in Stockholm, outside the regions Polymarket geoblocks; the other three run in Ashburn, Virginia.

3. Fixture discovery and matching

The two venues list the same match under different names, at different times, with different market structures. Polymarket lists a match as an event with one Yes/No market per outcome (“Will Borussia Dortmund win?”); Kalshi lists an event with one market per outcome, identified by team codes in the ticker. Kalshi’s listed time for club soccer is the expected end of the match, kickoff + 3 h, for all 116 club fixtures checked on 2026-10-06, so the matcher subtracts 3 h before comparing.

The matcher is a C++ port of the research matcher, without the parts that need a paid match-data API. It runs in execution at start-up and every 10 minutes; a failed round keeps the last fixture list.

Rule Setting
Competitions Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1, Champions League: the six of the part 3 replay, filtered by Kalshi series (KXEPLGAME, …) only, since a fixture must be listed on both venues anyway
Kickoff window ±6 h, after Kalshi’s 3 h correction
Team score Best of token subset, Jaccard and sequence ratio after an alias table for the six leagues’ clubs; women, reserve and B-team qualifiers must agree; home and away straight or swapped
Accept Fixture score ≥ 0.86 with the weaker team ≥ 0.72; or kickoff within 4 h, score ≥ 0.79 and weaker team ≥ 0.64
Ambiguity No match when the top two candidates score within 0.015, unless both are the same pair listed twice
Outcome mapping Kalshi by ticker suffix code; Polymarket by question similarity ≥ 0.6, with a NO token present

There is no accept step. Enable is the human check. Before a fixture is enabled, its row shows both venues’ raw titles, the kickoff gap between them and both venues’ prices, so a mismatch is visible at a glance.

The quoting clock is Polymarket’s scheduled start. The actual kickoff, half-time, goals and red cards are recorded from Kalshi’s free live_data feed, polled every 5 s from 15 minutes before kickoff. That feed runs about 53 s behind its own market at goals, which is fine for the record and is why it does not drive quoting.

4. Order books: from venue socket to decision

All the strategy reads from market data is, per outcome and venue, a best bid and a best ask with our own orders removed, and the age of the book they came from.

One book per venue and outcome

maker_maker subscribes to the YES instrument only. On Kalshi the YES market’s book already carries both sides. On Polymarket the NO token’s book mirrors the YES book (part 3), so subscribing to it would add traffic and a second book that can lag the first, with no new information. Orders still go to the NO instrument where a leg buys NO; their prices are read off the YES book, because a NO bid at x is a YES ask at 1 − x.

Leg Order sent to Order price Read from the YES book
pm_yes Polymarket YES token b Polymarket best bid, ex-self
pm_no Polymarket NO token 1 − a Polymarket best ask, ex-self
k_no Kalshi market, NO side 1 − ak Kalshi best ask, ex-self

Freshness

A book changes only when someone acts, so silence is ambiguous: it can be half-time or a dead connection. The replay pulled orders when a snapshot was older than 10 s, but that number was the cadence of the historical recorder. The live rule is built in layers, so that silence downstream always means a problem:

Layer Rule Purpose
Venue socket Client ping every 2 s (Polymarket text PING, Kalshi WebSocket ping); the socket is alive while any frame arrived in the last 5 s Proves liveness on a quiet book without waiting for Kalshi’s ~10 s server pings
Book validity A snapshot since the last connect; on Kalshi, no sequence gap since A book that may have lost a message is invalid, never silently wrong
Engine re-send Every valid book is re-sent once a second while its socket is alive A quiet market still produces one message a second
Execution Book age on execution’s monotonic clock; when either book of an outcome is 3 s old, cancel all three legs Measures the whole venue → engine → execution path; 3 s sits inside Kalshi’s 3–6 s lead over Polymarket
Venue backstop Polymarket’s heartbeat cancels all open orders 10–15 s after the last refresh; Kalshi has no equivalent Covers a dead execution process or network; on Kalshi, L = 10 bounds what can rest

Cancelling all three legs on either stale book is deliberate: the Polymarket legs need Kalshi’s book for the guard, and k_no needs Polymarket’s bid for its edge check. A closed connection invalidates its books at once and they are pulled 3 s later. A silent stall takes up to 5 s to detect in the engine, then 3 s in execution.

Kalshi numbers every message on a subscription with one sequence across all its markets, acknowledgements included, so a gap cannot be pinned to one market. A gap invalidates every book on the subscription and requests fresh snapshots in place, keeping the fill channel on the same socket up; a second gap within 5 s, a failed snapshot or a terminal error reconnects. Markets are also added and removed in place: in a regression probe, 30 of 30 books kept streaming through market-set changes, with a longest gap of 1.19 s.

Removing our own orders

The strategy joins the best price of everyone else. If it priced against a book that includes its own order, the order would peg to itself and stay behind when the rest of the market moves. Execution therefore subtracts its own resting size at each level before reading the top of the book. Our NO bids at 1 − p count as YES asks at p.

The subtraction has a race: just after an order is placed, the venue may still send a book that does not show it. The rule is: others = displayed − own when displayed ≥ own, otherwise displayed. For a pm_no order resting as a YES ask of 10 contracts at 47 c:

Book at 47 c Displayed Ours Others Ex-self best ask Result
Others also rest at 47 c 300 10 290 47 c Stay
Everyone else has left 47 c 10 10 0 48 c Move to 48 c
Book not yet showing our order 5 10 5 47 c Stay; no false move

5. The decision core

One thread, one queue

One strategy thread owns all trading state and drains one queue. Blocking calls run on worker pools, so a place and a cancel can be in flight together, and their responses come back as events. Each batch ends with decide and reconciliation for every active fixture. Because state changes only on this thread and every decision is recorded, a fixture can later be replayed through the same code.

decide: the rules as a pure function

decide is the port of the replay’s quoting step, and it stays pure: the same input gives the same targets. Its input is, per outcome, the two ex-self tops of book with their ages and q, plus cash spent per venue, time since kickoff and the configuration. Its output is, per outcome and leg, a price and size or a reason for not quoting. Reasons are checked in this order and recorded with every decision:

Scope Reason Condition
Outcome disabled, before_kickoff, window_end Fixture not quoting; before scheduled kickoff; 104 minutes after it
Outcome stale_book Either book 3 s old or more
Outcome invalid_book Either book missing a side, or crossed
Outcome price_band Polymarket mid outside 5–95 c
Leg leg_off Switched off in the configuration
k_no not_post_only Kalshi ask at or below Kalshi bid
k_no no_edge ak − b − Kalshi maker fee ≤ 0
pm_yes, pm_no guard b ≥ Kalshi ask, or a ≤ Kalshi bid (plus the buffer)
Leg inventory Target size rounds to zero contracts
Leg cap Size reduced by the cash cap

The cash cap is per venue and fixture, shared by both outcomes. Cash used is cash paid for positions plus collateral of resting orders, and without merges a Polymarket YES + NO pair stays locked until settlement. When the cap binds, orders that reduce an outcome’s |q| are funded first and the opening orders share what is left: the strategy stops opening before it stops hedging.

The reconciler

The touch is only one of the reasons orders change: fills change q and therefore sizes, and the guard, the cap and the window add or remove legs. Instead of one rule per trigger, the reconciler diffs the desired orders against the live ones after every event and issues the fewest writes. Each leg is a stack of up to four child orders at one price.

Situation Kalshi Polymarket
Touch moved Amend the oldest child to the new price and size; cancel the others Cancel and re-post at once; the old order counts toward cash until its cancel is confirmed
Leg too large Amend the youngest child down; it keeps its queue priority Cancel the youngest children
Leg too small New child at the back of the queue, at least 1 contract New child at the back, at least 5 contracts
Leg must not quote Cancel at once, unthrottled Cancel at once, unthrottled

Writes are budgeted. A leg reprices at most once per 500 ms, a leg with a request in flight gets no second one, and a token bucket holds each venue under its write limit (15 writes/s on Polymarket, 8 on Kalshi, whose basic tier allows about 10); cancels always pass. Kalshi’s amend has no post-only flag, so an amend priced through the book would fill as a taker; the not_post_only check guards against it, and every fill records whether it was a taker.

Unknown orders are resolved against the venue’s open orders every 2 s. Every 30 s all live orders are checked, which catches orders the venue cancelled on its own; orders on our instruments that execution does not track, seen in two sweeps in a row, are cancelled.

6. Fills, inventory and safety

Fills arrive on each engine’s fill stream: Kalshi’s fill channel and Polymarket’s user channel. Execution deduplicates them by venue and fill id, updates the outcome’s holdings, q and cash, and the next decide resizes the legs. A fill also schedules a window of market data around it for the record (section 7).

Inventory is never assumed. Thirty seconds before kickoff, a fixture registers its instruments again, cancels every open order on them and reads positions and balances from both venues, so a restart mid-match continues from the true inventory. A lost fill stream restarts every quoting fixture the same way, because fills may have been missed while it was down. P&L counts fills since the fixture started, marked at Kalshi’s mid.

Trigger Scope Action
Either book 3 s old Outcome Pull the three legs; quote again on the next fresh book
Guard Leg Pull the Polymarket leg
104 minutes after kickoff Fixture Pull all legs
Inventory reaches L either way Leg Legs that would add to it size to zero; no order exceeds 3L
Mark-to-market loss below −$40 (replay worst: −$17) Fixture Stop the fixture
Order outcome unknown Leg Freeze until resolved
Fill stream lost Quoting fixtures Restart from venue positions
Engine unhealthy for 90 s (checked every 5 s) All Disable every fixture and cancel
No management heartbeat for 120 s (sent every 15 s) All Disable every fixture and cancel
Two failed Polymarket heartbeats All Disable every fixture and cancel
Kill switch All Disable every fixture and sweep every open order
Execution or network dies Venue Polymarket cancels within 10–15 s; Kalshi orders keep resting, at most L = 10 per leg

Only the pulls at the top of the table resume by themselves. A stopped or disabled fixture trades again only when it is enabled again by hand. maker_maker is the only module trading its accounts.

7. The trading record

The record keeps only what the replay comparison needs and what cannot be downloaded later. It is one SQLite file per UTC day on the execution volume. The strategy thread enqueues rows and a single writer thread writes them, so trading never waits on disk, and a full disk drops rows without stopping trading.

Table One row per Answers
decisions Order action, with kind (new, top-up, shrink, cancel, re-post, amend) and reason Why each order changed
orders Request, ack or reject, cancel, amend and fill, with exchange, engine and execution times Latency, rejects, fills, fees, taker fills
state Decision Both books, q, holdings, cash headroom, guard state and book ages at that moment
queue Resting order, when it ends Displayed size at our price on ack, volume traded there, shrink from cancels ahead: the realized queue share ρ
kalshi_queue Kalshi order, every 5 s and 1 s after a trade at our price Kalshi’s own count of contracts ahead, to check queue
books_1s Traded market and second, in play Best price plus four levels per side: the input of the replay
trades Public print on a traded market Fill types and markouts
fill_windows Book message or trade from 5 s before to 30 s after each fill, both venues What happened around every fill
match_events Change in Kalshi’s match state Actual kickoff, half-time, goals, red cards
fixtures Fixture start Configuration, code commit, balances

The queue estimate turns the test into a measurement of ρ, the largest lever in the replay. Each order starts behind the displayed size at its price; trades at that price move it forward, and a level that shrinks below it means cancellations ahead. Every filled order then yields the share of the displayed queue that actually traded ahead of it, and on Kalshi the venue’s own queue position checks the estimate.

Full ladders in books_1s and fill_windows cost about 120–220 MB per fixture, estimated from measured message rates (busiest market: 28 messages/s on Kalshi, 21 on Polymarket). The 10 GB volume holds 40 fixtures or more.

8. The control page

The page is served by management and talks only to execution. Fixtures sit in three tables: Live (from kickoff until a venue closes the market), Upcoming (soonest first, with a divider after the next 24 h) and Finished (kept 48 h, with the fixture’s P&L). Every row shows the league, the kickoff gap between venues, both venues’ titles with links, the match score from Kalshi’s feed, an Enable toggle, and for home and away the YES best bid and ask with size on each venue.

Rows that are not traded take their prices from GetOrderbookSnapshots every 45 s, one batched REST read per venue. Display never drives the trading subscriptions: each engine keeps one market set and the latest request replaces it, so display rows would add and drop markets under the traded books.

9. Deployment and operations

Service Region Machine Swap Volume Measured memory
engine-pm Stockholm shared CPU, 1 GB 512 MB – 100–570 MB
engine-kalshi Ashburn shared CPU, 512 MB 512 MB – 110–330 MB
execution Ashburn shared CPU, 512 MB – 10 GB peak 35 MB
management Ashburn shared CPU, 512 MB – – peak 80 MB

Sizes come from a local load test: all four processes against the real venues for 16 minutes, 40 markets streamed per engine (8–10 times one fixture), and the control page polled every second. Seven days cost about $5: $4.10 compute, $0.35 for the volume, and cents of transfer.

Logs go to standard output, where Fly keeps them searchable for 7 days. Every line starts with its level; notable events carry a fixed prefix (mm fixture=<id> leg=<leg> <event>), and engines log freshness as transitions only (ws venue=kalshi event=dead silent_ms=...), so a stale-book pull in execution lines up with one engine line. Routine quotes are in the record, not the logs. There is no alerting in v1: someone watches every enabled match.

Two problems from the first deployment

Listings that outgrew the machine. On the first Fly run, every Polymarket listing hit its deadline. The engine paged Gamma’s /events/keyset, which returns every derivative market of every soccer event. On a throttled shared CPU it ran past the 90 s deadline and kept working after the caller gave up, so calls piled up: 821 MB of memory, a load of 2.6, and engine health flapping.

Polymarket listing /events/keyset /markets/keyset, moneyline filter
Downloaded per call 929 MB 24 MB
Time per call Past the 90 s deadline on Fly About 10 s and 3 CPU-seconds, locally
Moneyline markets found 3,852 3,852

The listing also stops between pages once its call is cancelled, and the matcher now waits up to 300 s.

Memory that only went up.

It was not a leak: engine-kalshi reached 356 MB resident plus 92 MB of swap after 4.5 hours while its memory in use stayed at 4–11 MB (Polymarket: 47–92 MB). Each market listing parses a large JSON catalog; glibc keeps freed memory in per-thread arenas (eight on a one-CPU machine), and the calls land on different gRPC threads, so every arena stays at its peak.

The fix is one call: each engine runs malloc_trim(0) once a minute and logs resident, in-use and held memory every 10 minutes. In a local A/B replaying about 16 hours of Kalshi and 46 hours of Polymarket listing traffic in 30 minutes, the old builds rose to 533 MB and 805 MB and kept rising; the trimmed builds stayed in the ranges of the table above. On Fly, engine-pm has held about 220 MB since. engine-kalshi’s baseline still drifted from about 100 to 200 MB over ten hours, inside the measured range, and the mem log line is the check.

10. Design decisions

Decision Instead of Because
Engine re-sends and a 3 s threshold Each leg’s age from venue messages, 10 s A quiet book is not a dead one, and Kalshi leads by 3–6 s
Kalshi amend for price and size Cancel and place; Kalshi’s decrease endpoint One atomic write under a ~10 writes/s limit; keeps priority on a size cut; the decrease endpoint leaves reduce_to undefined
Desired-state reconciler One rule per trigger Fills, the guard, the cap and the window change orders too
Own C++ matcher Exact-name mapping; a paid match-data API Club names differ across venues; no Python in the live path
Real orders from the first fixture A shadow mode Queue position and latency exist only for real orders; L = 10 bounds the cost
No Polymarket merges in v1 Merging each pair at once Relayer support for the account’s wallet type is unverified; the Polymarket cap was raised to $300 instead

11. Status and next steps

Milestone Status
1. Engine changes: amend, book snapshots, re-sends, pings, sequence-gap recovery, Polymarket heartbeat chaining Done. Probed against both venues on 2026-10-06, including a real 1 c post-only order on each, amended on Kalshi, then cancelled

Known gaps in v1:

  • No Kalshi order group yet, so there is no venue-side contract limit on Kalshi; L = 10 is the bound.
  • A Polymarket trade that fails on chain is logged, but its fill is not reversed.
  • Trades on Polymarket’s NO book are not streamed, so the queue estimate undercounts volume for pm_no; Kalshi’s queue position is exact.
  • The page’s P&L is marked at Kalshi’s mid, not at settlement, and settled Polymarket positions are redeemed by hand.
  • Still to be seen on the first live fixture: no false staleness at half-time.

Not in v1, because it is not replayed yet or not needed to measure: draws, pregame quoting, joint inventory across the two outcomes, taker flattening after a timeout, and Polymarket merges.

The results will be published here once enough fixtures have been traded to compare the live fills with the replay.
